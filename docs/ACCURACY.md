# Accuracy and evidence

This ledger records what Riptide's movement physics are checked against. Nothing
here was captured from a live Bedrock client during this work. Claims are
limited to the evidence listed.

## Evidence levels

`Result.confidence()` reports the weakest level among the mechanics a tick used:

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `decompiled` | BedSim cites native client disassembly for the constants or ordering. |
| `ported` | Matches BedSim/Oomph bit for bit, without cited native evidence. |
| `uncertain` | Open questions remain, or the behaviour depends on caller-supplied rules. |

## Verified against real Bedrock

- **Wall contact rounding.** Two player positions captured from a real BDS
  session (from BedSim's `bbox_test.go`) are reproduced exactly by the f32 box
  construction and `AABB::clipCollide` port (`src/tests.zig`, "captured BDS
  wall contacts").
- **Constants with cited disassembly** (cited by BedSim, not re-verified here):
  the 1e-6 clip contact epsilon (Lens 26.30), soul sand's 1.225 acceleration
  friction multiplier and the ground acceleration operation order (Lens
  1.26.50.26), the sprint movement modifier (Lens 1.26.50.26 and a BDS
  1.26.45.1 UpdateAttributes packet), the 0.025 sneak edge inset (1.16 China
  binary), and the 0.2 ladder speed (`Mob::ascendLadder`).

## Verified against BedSim

`tests/fixtures/bedsim.json` holds 188 scenarios (8,930 ticks) generated by
`tools/oracle` from BedSim 9c12b344 with one patch (below). Every tick must
match bit for bit: outcome, position, velocity, displacement, ground and
collision flags, fall distance, pose, height, jump delay, and Riptide timer.
The test also fails if any tracked mechanic goes unexercised.

Covered: walking, strafing, sprinting, sprint jumping (modern and legacy sprint
timing), ice, blue ice, slime, beds, soul sand with and without Soul Speed,
honey (walking, jumping, wall slides, carpet on honey), stepping on slabs and
stairs, steps during jumps, walls, fences, wall posts, head bumps, sneak edges,
Swift Sneak, sneaking under ceilings, crawling, ladders, vines, scaffolding
(static and player-dependent shapes), cobwebs, Weaving, sweet berry bushes,
powder snow with and without leather boots, water (idle, swimming, pitch
steering, start/stop swimming, exit boosts, Depth Strider, Dolphin's Grace,
currents, falling columns, waterlogged blocks), lava, bubble columns, Elytra
gliding (pitch, boost, walls, landing, levitation cancel), Riptide (airborne
and grounded), knockback on land and in water, instant and smoothed teleports,
item-use slowdown (processed and raw input), Jump Boost, Levitation, Slow
Falling, depenetration, long falls, and 120 seeded random worlds with random
input. The oracle regenerates byte-identical fixtures on Windows and Linux,
and the fixtures pass on both in Debug, ReleaseSafe, ReleaseFast and
ReleaseSmall, including a baseline x86-64 CPU build.

## Deliberate differences from BedSim

| Change | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| `moveRelative` uses correctly rounded `sinf`/`cosf` (f64 then one rounding). | BedSim notes the client uses `sincosf`. Mainstream libm `sinf` implementations (glibc, FreeBSD msun and Bionic) are correctly rounded or within about half an ulp. BedSim's `math32.Sin`/`Cos` is a float32 Cephes port that differs from a correctly rounded result on about 36% and 45% of yaw angles. Across the fixture scenarios this changes 119 of 188 scenarios (28% of ticks, at most 1.5e-6 position drift within 120 ticks). The oracle carries the same patch (`tools/oracle/oracle.patch`). |
| Queued motion (knockback, SetActorMotion) also applies while gliding. | BedSim drops it on gliding ticks. A server-set velocity vanishing is an omission, not client behaviour. Flagged under `motion` and `glide`. |
| Firework boosts are armed by `State.queueFireworkBoost()`. | BedSim cannot start a boost (BedSim PR #30 adds input plumbing). A rocket is a server-validated event, not a client flag. |
| Swim retention is `Policy.swim_grace`: `.trust_client` (vanilla) or a tick budget (hardened). | BedSim always hardens. Riptide keeps vanilla behaviour available and makes the anticheat rule explicit. |
| The step client tiebreak is `Policy.step_client_tiebreak`, off in both presets. | It is reconciliation policy, not client physics. |
| Unknown blocks are detected per lookup. | Every consulted cell must be loaded, which is stricter and simpler than BedSim's chunk-range pre-checks. Outcome ordering can differ at load boundaries. |
| Huge sweeps and coordinates return `query_limit`. | Bounds work on hostile input. BedSim reports them as unloaded. |
| Player-dependent shapes also apply to support and standing-on probes. | BedSim needs a separate provider for this. |
| Zero gravity and zero liquid speeds are respected. | BedSim treats zero as "unset". Riptide has explicit defaults. |
| Legacy slide offset and compatibility options are removed. | Clients from 1.20.60 on do not use the slide offset (Oomph). |

## Open questions and unsupported mechanics

- **Creative and spectator flight, no-clip, flying**: no flight equations exist
  in BedSim, Oomph or their history, so these ticks are `client_authoritative`.
- **Vehicles**: mounted ticks are `mounted` and follow the reported state.
- **Bounce gravity**: BedSim PR #35 proposes partial gravity on slime and bed
  rebound ticks. It is unverified, so it is not applied.
- **Teleports**: Oomph runs physics after the final smoothed-teleport snap and
  allows a jump on an instant-teleport tick. BedSim does neither, and Riptide
  follows BedSim.
- **Item-use slowdown** is one generic modifier. Per-item rules are not known
  precisely. Callers can pass `Input.item_use_modifier`.
- **Floating-point platforms**: physics assume strict per-operation f32
  rounding (x86-64 clients). ARM clients built with FMA contraction may differ
  by an ulp. A client libm `sinf` that is not correctly rounded may also differ.
- **Pistons and entity collision** are not modelled. Worlds can supply moving
  shapes through `dynamic_shape`, but push ordering is not simulated.
- **Scaffolding and powder snow collision rules** are supplied by the world's
  `dynamic_shape` callback. They are not built in, because their exact Bedrock
  rules are undocumented.
- **Liquid flow through non-liquid neighbours** uses the world's `solid_faces`
  and `flow_barrier`. Material permeability beyond that is not modelled.
